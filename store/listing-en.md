# Chrome Web Store listing — English (default)

## Name
Turkish Suffix Separator

## Summary (from the manifest, 107/132 characters)
Hover a Turkish word to see its root and every suffix, with what each one means. Works offline, no account.

## Category
Education

## Description
Turkish builds long words by stacking suffixes onto a root. A single word like "gidemeyeceklermiş" carries a whole English sentence: "apparently they won't be able to go." Turkish Suffix Separator takes these words apart for you, right on the page you are reading.

Rest your mouse on any Turkish word and a small card shows:
• The word split into its root and suffixes, each in its own color: gid · eme · yecek · ler · miş
• The English meaning of the root (git- = to go)
• What each suffix adds, in plain English: cannot, will, they, apparently
• The grammar term in English and Turkish, for example "future tense - gelecek zaman"
• Other possible readings, when a word can be read more than one way

WHO IT IS FOR
• Learners of Turkish at any level who read news, blogs, Wikipedia or books online
• Teachers who want to show how a Turkish word is built
• Anyone who has looked at a long Turkish word and wondered where to start

HOW TO USE IT
• On Turkish web pages, just rest the mouse on a word. On other pages, hold Alt while hovering.
• Click the toolbar icon to choose when the card appears, how quickly it opens, and whether grammar terms are shown in English, Turkish or both.
• Press Esc to close the card.

PRIVATE AND OFFLINE
Everything happens inside your browser. The analyzer is rule-based and uses a built-in dictionary of Turkish roots, so there is no server, no AI service and no internet connection needed. The extension does not collect, store or send any personal or browsing data. No account, no ads, no tracking.

GOOD TO KNOW
The analyzer covers noun and verb endings, the copula (-dir, -miş, -di), participles, voice suffixes (passive, causative, reciprocal) and common derivational suffixes. It looks at one word at a time, not the whole sentence, so when a word has several readings it shows the most likely one first and lists the others. Words it cannot analyze are marked "Not recognized" instead of being guessed.
